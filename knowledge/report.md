# Meeting report — guidance for the agent

This file is read when the report is generated. Edit it to change the report's content
and tone. All figures in the report are computed from the recorded data; this guidance
only shapes the wording.

## Purpose

The banker reads this report shortly before meeting the client. It must be quick to scan
and give the banker natural ways to bring up each point.

## Structure

1. **Situation in brief.** Three or four sentences: who the client is, the shape of their
   wealth (what dominates, where it sits), and the family situation. No figure that is not
   in the data.
2. **Meeting points**, in this order: wealth, family, general. Within each group, the
   points that most affect the client's situation come first; missing details come last.
3. For each meeting point, a **conversation opener**: one or two sentences the banker
   could say to the client more or less as written.

## Writing conversation openers

- Open, friendly questions. Start from the client's life, not from the bank's file.
  "How are things going with the Mallorca apartment — are you using it as much as you
  hoped?" rather than "We need the financing details of your Spanish property."
- One topic per opener.
- Never reveal that something is "missing in our records" in a way that makes the client
  feel audited. For a missing detail, ask the question that naturally yields it.
- For an inconsistency, ask for an update rather than pointing out the contradiction:
  "Last time we spoke you mentioned holdings with other banks — has anything changed
  there?"
- Merge closely related points into a single opener when one question covers both.
- Plain language. No jargon, no product names.

## What never to do

- No recommendation of a product, an allocation or a transaction.
- No tax or legal conclusion. Where relevant, suggest offering the client a conversation
  with a specialist.
- No figure, name or fact that is not in the recorded data.
