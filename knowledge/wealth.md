# Wealth review — guidance for the agent

This file is read by the agent during the wealth stage. Edit it to change what the agent
asks about and what it flags. Hard checks (missing fields, arithmetic) run in code and do
not need to be repeated here.

## What a complete picture contains

**Assets.** For each one: value, currency, type, country, and whether it sits in a bank
custody account (bankable). For bankable assets, also the custodian bank.

- Bankable: cash, equities, bonds, funds, structured products.
- Non-bankable: real estate, business stakes, private equity, art and collectibles.
- Either: crypto (exchange or bank custody vs self-custody), precious metals (account vs
  physical), insurance policies. Ask.

"Country" means where the asset is located or booked: the property's location, the
company's seat, the country of the custodian bank.

**Liabilities.** Mortgages, Lombard loans, personal and business loans, tax owed. For each:
amount, currency, lender, country, and for a mortgage which property it finances.

**Pension plans.** Occupational pension (second pillar), private pension (pillar 3a/3b),
foreign plans. These are off balance sheet.

**Income.** Recurring (salary, dividends, rental, pension, business income) per year, and
one-off (bonus, inheritance, gift, sale of an asset) with the expected date.

**Expenses.** Recurring (living costs, housing, taxes, education, alimony, debt service)
per year, and one-off (major purchase, gift, donation) with the date.

## Situations that deserve a question for the client

Raise these as meeting points when they apply. Phrase each as something to explore with
the client, not as a conclusion.

- **Real estate abroad.** How is it financed and held (personally, through a company)?
  Is it rented out? Who uses it? Local inheritance rules may differ from Swiss ones.
- **Assets or family in the United States.** US-situs assets (US property, US shares held
  directly) and US-resident family members raise US tax and estate questions. Suggest
  involving the bank's cross-border specialists.
- **Assets booked with other banks.** What role does each bank play for the client? Is
  there a reason for the split?
- **Business stake.** What share of total wealth depends on the company? Is there a
  succession or exit plan, and a timeline? How regular is the dividend?
- **Employer shares and stock options.** Vesting schedule, lock-ups, and how much of the
  client's wealth and income depend on one employer.
- **Crypto and physical precious metals.** How are they held and who else knows how to
  access them?
- **Currency mismatch.** Large assets or debts in a currency other than the one the client
  spends in.
- **Lombard loan.** What is it used for, and how does the client see it being repaid?
- **Upcoming one-off inflows** (bonus, inheritance, sale). What does the client intend to
  do with the money, and when?
- **Planned large outflows** (gifts, purchases). Timing and how they will be funded.
- **Expenses above recurring income.** Not a problem in itself for a client living off
  capital, but confirm how the gap is funded and for how long.
- **No liabilities at all on a property owner.** Confirm the property is debt-free.
- **Nearing retirement.** From the mid-fifties: pension situation, planned retirement
  date, lump sum or annuity.

## Plausibility

Query the banker when a figure looks out of line, for example:

- An amount far outside the rest of the dossier (a salary in the tens of millions, a
  property worth a few thousand).
- Living costs that seem very low or very high for the wealth and the family size.
- A mortgage larger than the value of the property it finances.
- A currency that does not fit the country (a Swiss mortgage in US dollars).

If the banker confirms, keep the figure. If the banker is unsure, record a meeting point.

## What never to do

- Never recommend a product, an allocation or a transaction. You help the banker prepare
  questions; the banker advises the client.
- Never state a tax or legal conclusion. Say that the topic should be explored, and with
  whom.
- Never invent a figure. If the banker does not know, record what is known and leave the
  rest empty.

## Activity notes and the CRM

What is on screen comes from the structured fields of the CRM. Bankers also write free-text
activity notes after each contact, and often leave information there without entering it in
the fields. When a note mentions something the fields do not carry, it appears as a meeting
point.

- Say where it comes from: "Your note of the twelfth of May mentions about four million with
  Julius Baer, and it is not in the CRM."
- Ask whether it is still right and whether to record it. Notes can be out of date.
- If the banker confirms, record it with the tools, as you would anything else they tell you.
  If they are not sure, keep it for the meeting.
- Never record what a note says without the banker confirming it.
