# Family review — guidance for the agent

This file is read by the agent during the family stage. Edit it to change what the agent
asks about and what it flags.

## What a complete picture contains

- **Marital status**: single, married, registered partnership, cohabiting, separated,
  divorced, widowed.
- **Spouse or partner**: name, age, where they live.
- **Former spouses** when there are children or ongoing obligations (alimony).
- **Children**: for each, name, age, where they live, and which partner is the other
  parent when the client has children from more than one relationship. If the client has
  no children, record that explicitly.
- **Anything else that matters**: a child with special needs, a dependent parent, a family
  member involved in the client's business, a strained relationship, a recent death,
  grandchildren the client wants to provide for.

The banker often gives ages rather than years of birth. Either is fine.

## Situations that deserve a question for the client

Raise these as meeting points when they apply. Phrase each as something to explore.

- **Married or in a partnership.** Which matrimonial property regime applies? Is there a
  marriage contract? Is the partner involved in financial decisions and should they join
  the meeting?
- **Cohabiting, not married.** An unmarried partner has no automatic inheritance rights
  or pension entitlement. Has the client made arrangements?
- **Divorced or separated.** Are there ongoing obligations (alimony, shared property)?
  Were pension assets split?
- **Children from more than one relationship.** How does the client want each to be
  treated? Is there a will or an inheritance contract?
- **Minor children.** Who would look after them and their assets if something happened to
  the client? Education plans and their cost.
- **Adult children.** Are they financially independent? Are gifts or advances on
  inheritance planned? Should the bank get to know the next generation?
- **Family members living abroad.** Different inheritance and tax rules may apply. US
  residents in particular: involve the cross-border specialists.
- **A child working in the family business.** Succession, and fairness towards siblings
  who do not.
- **Widowed.** Is the late spouse's estate settled? Has the client updated their own
  arrangements since?
- **Client aged 70 or more.** Is there a power of attorney or an advance care directive?
  Who is the trusted contact?

## Plausibility

- A child whose age does not fit the client's age.
- A spouse recorded for a client whose status is single, divorced or widowed.
- Children recorded when the client is said to have none.

Query the banker; if they are unsure, record a meeting point.

## What never to do

- Never give legal advice on succession or matrimonial law. Flag the topic and suggest
  the client speak to a specialist.
- Never record sensitive remarks about family members (health, conflicts) beyond what the
  banker explicitly asks you to note.
- Never invent a name, an age or a place.

## Activity notes and the CRM

What is on screen comes from the structured fields of the CRM. When one of the banker's
free-text activity notes mentions a family fact the fields do not carry (a marriage, a child,
a move abroad), say which note it is, ask whether it is still right, and record it only once
the banker confirms.
